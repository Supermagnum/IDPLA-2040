# IDPLA-2040

# INTERNATIONAL DIGITAL PRIVACY AND LAWFUL ACCESS TREATY
## IDPLA-2040

*Open for Signature, Geneva, 1 January 2035 — In Force, 1 January 2038*

---

## PREAMBLE

The Signatory States, hereinafter referred to as "the Parties",

Recognising that the dignity and autonomy of every person is inseparable from the right to control information generated by or derived from their body, behaviour, and communications;

Affirming that the rule of law requires, in exceptional and strictly defined circumstances, lawful access to certain categories of data for the prevention and prosecution of the most serious crimes;

Acknowledging the particular sensitivity of data derived from non-invasive neural-reading devices, biometric systems, satellite remote sensing, voice-pattern analysis technologies, and internet behavioural profiling systems;

Recognising that end-to-end encryption is an essential safeguard for professional communications in the financial, medical, legal, and related sectors, and that its integrity must be preserved by law;

Determined to establish a framework that is technologically neutral, forward-looking, and enforceable across jurisdictions;

Have agreed as follows:

---

## PART I — GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Article 1 — Definitions

For the purposes of this Treaty:

1. **Personal Data** means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.
2. **Neural Data** means any signal, inference, or derived output originating from a non-invasive device that reads electrical, optical, or magnetic activity associated with the human nervous system.
3. **Neural-Reading Device** means any wearable or external apparatus capable of detecting neural signals without surgical implantation or physical breach of the skin, operating exclusively in a read-only mode with no capacity to transmit signals into the body.
4. **Voice Profile** means a biometric representation of an individual's vocal characteristics sufficient to identify or re-identify that person across recordings, irrespective of content.
5. **Satellite Imagery** means visual or multispectral data captured by orbital or sub-orbital platforms depicting the surface of the Earth.
6. **Crimes Against Humanity** has the meaning established under the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court, as amended.
7. **Competent Authority** means the independent judicial or quasi-judicial body designated by each Party to authorise lawful access under this Treaty.
8. **Oversight Panel** means the international body established under Article 14 of this Treaty.
9. **Behavioural Profile** means any structured representation of an individual's online activities, preferences, associations, or inferred characteristics, derived from the aggregation of internet usage data across one or more platforms or services.
10. **Cross-Platform Identity Stitching** means the linking of identifiers, behavioural patterns, or data records originating from two or more distinct digital services or platforms for the purpose of constructing or enriching a Behavioural Profile.
11. **Certified End-to-End Encryption** means a cryptographic scheme, conforming to an open and publicly auditable standard such as OpenPGP/GnuPG or a successor standard approved by the Technology Review Board, in which message content is rendered unintelligible to all parties other than the intended sender and recipient, and in which no intermediary holds or can be compelled to produce a decryption key.
12. **Sensitive Professional Communication** means any transmission of data between or among financial service providers and their clients, licensed medical practitioners and their patients or professional peers, legal counsel and their clients, notaries, social workers, and equivalent regulated professionals operating under duties of confidentiality.
13. **AI Flagging System** means any automated system employing machine learning, pattern recognition, statistical inference, or related techniques to identify individuals, communications, or behaviours as warranting investigative attention by a public authority.
14. **Human Review Officer** means a qualified human analyst, holding defined professional credentials and security clearance, designated to independently evaluate flags generated by an AI Flagging System before any investigative action is taken.

### Article 2 — Scope

This Treaty applies to all natural persons within the jurisdiction of any Party and to all data processing entities operating under the law of any Party, regardless of where processing takes place physically.

### Article 3 — Foundational Principles

All data processing subject to this Treaty shall comply with the following principles:

- **Purpose limitation**: data collected for one defined purpose may not be repurposed without fresh legal basis and, where applicable, fresh consent.
- **Proportionality**: any interference with privacy rights must be no greater than strictly necessary to achieve the legitimate aim pursued.
- **Temporal limitation**: all retained personal data must be subject to defined deletion schedules, enforceable by automated cryptographic means where technically feasible.
- **Accountability**: every entity processing personal data bears the burden of demonstrating compliance; the absence of harm does not constitute compliance.

## PART II — PROTECTED DATA CATEGORIES

### Article 4 — Neural Data

1. Neural Data shall constitute an absolutely protected category. No Party shall permit the collection, storage, transmission, or processing of Neural Data except for purposes expressly consented to by the data subject, in writing, on a per-session basis.
2. Neural-Reading Devices shall, by certified design, operate in one direction only: reading outward. No device may be approved for use under this Treaty that retains any capacity to transmit, stimulate, or influence neural activity.
3. Raw neural signals shall be processed exclusively on the data subject's personal device. Only anonymised, aggregated, or functionally derived outputs — such as attention state or cognitive load indicators — may leave the device perimeter, and only with active per-session authorisation by the data subject.
4. Neural Data shall not be admissible as evidence in any criminal, civil, or administrative proceeding under the jurisdiction of any Party, nor shall it be accessible by any law enforcement or intelligence authority under any circumstances, including under Article 12 of this Treaty.
5. Manufacturers of Neural-Reading Devices shall be required to publish firmware in open-source, auditable form prior to receiving type-approval in any Party's jurisdiction. Concealed data exfiltration shall constitute a criminal offence under national implementing legislation.

### Article 5 — Biometric and Voice Data

1. Voice Profiles and other biometric identifiers shall be treated as sensitive personal data subject to explicit consent requirements and strict purpose limitation.
2. Passive collection of voice or biometric data in public spaces — without the knowledge of the data subject — is prohibited except as provided in Article 12.
3. No employer, insurer, educational institution, or provider of public or private services may condition access to employment, insurance, education, or services upon the provision of a Voice Profile or biometric identifier, unless such requirement is mandated by law and subject to independent judicial oversight.

### Article 6 — Satellite Imagery

1. Satellite Imagery depicting public spaces, land surfaces, coastlines, and territorial waters may be retained for long-term storage for the following purposes:
   - historical archiving and cultural heritage documentation;
   - environmental monitoring, climate science, and disaster response;
   - urban and land-use planning by public authorities;
   - navigation, cartography, and open geographic information systems.
2. Satellite Imagery that can identify or re-identify individual natural persons — by facial feature, gait, or other biometric marker visible at resolution — shall be subject to the same protections as biometric data under Article 5 and may not be retained in identifiable form beyond 90 days unless a Competent Authority has issued a specific retention order.
3. Commercial operators of satellite platforms shall file annual transparency reports with the Oversight Panel detailing imagery categories retained, jurisdictions covered, and any requests for access received from public authorities.

## PART III — LAWFUL ACCESS

### Article 7 — General Conditions for Lawful Access

1. Access by public authorities to personal data held by private entities shall require prior authorisation by a Competent Authority, except in the circumstances defined in Article 8.
2. Authorisation shall be:
   - specific as to the subject, the data category, and the time period;
   - proportionate to the severity of the offence under investigation;
   - time-limited, with automatic expiry no later than 90 days unless renewed;
   - subject to mandatory notification to the data subject upon conclusion of the investigation, unless a further judicial order for continued confidentiality is obtained.
3. Bulk collection of personal data — the indiscriminate gathering of data pertaining to persons not individually identified — is prohibited under all circumstances.

### Article 8 — Emergency Access

In circumstances of imminent threat to life, a Competent Authority may issue an emergency access order without prior hearing. Such orders shall be subject to retrospective judicial review within 48 hours and shall expire automatically if not confirmed by a full authorisation within that period.

### Article 9 — Cross-Border Requests

Requests by the authority of one Party for data held in the jurisdiction of another Party shall be governed by applicable mutual legal assistance agreements and shall, in all cases, comply with the standards of this Treaty. No Party shall be required to provide access that would violate the provisions of Part II.

## PART IV — INTERNET PROFILING, ENCRYPTION, AND AI OVERSIGHT

### Article 10a — Prohibition on Behavioural Profiling

1. The construction, maintenance, or commercial exploitation of Behavioural Profiles is prohibited without the freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous consent of the data subject, granted separately for each distinct purpose for which the profile is to be used.
2. Cross-Platform Identity Stitching by any state actor, commercial entity, or their agents is prohibited without prior authorisation by a Competent Authority issued under the conditions of Article 7. Commercial Cross-Platform Identity Stitching without judicial authorisation shall constitute a violation of this Treaty regardless of whether the individual data sources were individually lawful.
3. The following categories of internet activity data shall be treated as sensitive personal data under this Treaty, subject to the same protections as biometric data under Article 5:
   - search query histories;
   - browsing and navigation patterns;
   - social graph data, including contact lists, group memberships, and interaction frequencies;
   - purchase histories and financial transaction patterns derived from online activity;
   - location data inferred from internet usage.
4. Automated inference of political opinion, religious belief, health status, sexual orientation, or ethnic origin from internet activity data is prohibited under all circumstances, whether by state or commercial actors, and regardless of consent.
5. Individuals shall have the right to request, at any time and without charge, a full accounting of any Behavioural Profile held about them, to correct inaccuracies, and to demand permanent deletion. Deletion requests shall be honoured within 30 days and confirmed in writing signed with Gnupg.

### Article 10b — Mandatory End-to-End Encryption for Sensitive Professional Communications

1. All Sensitive Professional Communications transmitted digitally shall be protected by Certified End-to-End Encryption. This obligation applies to:
   - banking institutions, payment service providers, investment firms, and insurance companies in all client-facing and inter-institutional digital communications;
   - licensed medical practitioners, hospitals, clinics, pharmacies, and health data processors transmitting patient data between professional peers, institutions, or systems;
   - legal counsel, notaries, and equivalent regulated professionals in all client communications;
   - social service agencies handling case data relating to identified individuals.
2. Compliance with paragraph 1 shall be achieved using implementations conforming to the OpenPGP standard (RFC 4880 or successor), GnuPG, or an equivalent open-source, publicly auditable cryptographic framework approved by the Technology Review Board. Proprietary encryption schemes that do not permit independent audit shall not satisfy this obligation.
3. No Party shall impose, or permit to be imposed, any obligation on providers of Certified End-to-End Encryption to introduce backdoors, key escrow mechanisms, ghost keys, or any other technical measure that would enable third-party decryption without the knowledge and consent of the communicating parties. Such obligations, if enacted under national law, shall be deemed incompatible with this Treaty.
4. The transmission of Sensitive Professional Communications in unencrypted or insufficiently encrypted form shall constitute a breach of professional duty under national implementing legislation and, where systemic, a breach of this Treaty.
5. Key management shall remain exclusively with the communicating parties. Service providers acting as intermediaries shall have no access to private keys and shall not be required to retain them.
6. Law enforcement access to Sensitive Professional Communications shall remain governed exclusively by Articles 7 and 8. The existence of Certified End-to-End Encryption shall not constitute grounds for seeking exceptional access outside those provisions.

### Article 10c — AI Flagging Systems: Human Review and Contextual Harm Threshold

1. No AI Flagging System operating within the jurisdiction of any Party may initiate, recommend, or contribute to any investigative action against an individual — including the opening of a file, the issuance of a surveillance order, or any form of data collection — without prior independent review and positive confirmation by a designated Human Review Officer.
2. The Human Review Officer's determination shall be:
   - made independently, without presumption in favour of the AI Flagging System's output;
   - documented in writing with stated reasoning;
   - subject to professional accountability, including disciplinary consequences for systematic rubber-stamping of AI outputs without genuine independent analysis.
3. Any flag generated by an AI Flagging System that is not actively confirmed by a Human Review Officer within 72 hours shall expire automatically. Upon expiry, all data gathered in connection with that flag shall be purged from investigative systems and may not be retained, transferred, or used as the basis for a subsequent flag relating to the same individual and the same conduct.
4. **Contextual Harm Threshold.** A Human Review Officer shall not confirm a flag unless the underlying content or behaviour, assessed in full context, discloses a credible indication of actual or imminent harm to an identified or identifiable person or group. The following, among others, shall be explicitly insufficient grounds for confirmation:
   - the use of words, phrases, or topics that pattern-match to sensitive categories when the evident context is innocent, including culinary discussion, hobbyist activity, academic inquiry, fiction, or casual conversation;
   - the expression of religious belief, the recitation of religious texts, or participation in religious community activity;
   - the use of metaphorical, idiomatic, or figurative language;
   - the discussion of historical events, news, or public affairs;
   - the use of privacy-preserving tools such as VPNs, encrypted messaging applications, or anonymisation services.
5. AI Flagging Systems shall be subject to mandatory independent auditing at intervals of no greater than 12 months. Systems recording a false positive confirmation rate — defined as flags confirmed by Human Review Officers that subsequently result in no further investigative action — exceeding 10% over any rolling 6-month period shall be suspended from operational use pending recalibration and re-certification by the Technology Review Board.
6. The deployment of any AI Flagging System by a public authority shall be notified to the Oversight Panel prior to operational use, with documentation of the system's design, training data categories, intended scope, and known limitations. Covert deployment without such notification shall constitute a serious breach of this Treaty.

## PART V — SPECIAL CLAUSE: CRIMES AGAINST HUMANITY

### Article 10 — Exceptional Collection Authority

1. Notwithstanding the general prohibitions of Part II and the standard requirements of Part III, a Competent Authority may authorise the targeted collection of Voice Profile data and associated communications metadata in cases where there is credible evidence that an identified or identifiable individual is engaged in conduct constituting, or directly preparatory to, Crimes Against Humanity.
2. Such authorisation shall require:
   - a written determination by the Competent Authority that the ordinary lawful access regime is insufficient given the nature of the threat;
   - independent confirmation by at least two senior judicial officers;
   - referral, within 14 days, to the Oversight Panel for review.

### Article 11 — Voice Identification in Evasion Contexts

1. Where an individual under authorisation pursuant to Article 10 is suspected of deliberately evading identification by using unregistered or prepaid telecommunications devices, a Competent Authority may authorise voice-matching operations across communications streams.
2. In recognition of the particular challenge posed by individuals who vary their speech register — including through the use of religious or liturgical language drawn from texts such as the Bible, the Qur'an, the Torah, or equivalent canonical sources, whether in original or translated form — voice-matching algorithms approved for use under this Article shall be certified to function reliably across:
   - formal and informal speech registers;
   - recited or memorised textual material;
   - multiple languages and dialects used by the same speaker.
3. Voice data collected under this Article shall be used solely for identification purposes in the context of the authorised investigation. It shall not be retained beyond the conclusion of proceedings, shall not be shared with third parties outside the chain of lawful authority, and shall be subject to destruction certification filed with the Oversight Panel.
4. The use of prepaid or unregistered devices by a suspect shall not, of itself, constitute evidence of guilt or intent; it may be considered as one contextual factor within a broader body of evidence.

### Article 12 — Safeguards Specific to Article 10–11 Operations

1. All collection operations conducted under Articles 10 and 11 shall be logged in a tamper-evident audit record accessible to the Oversight Panel in real time.
2. Any misuse of Article 10–11 authorities for purposes other than those specified — including domestic political surveillance, targeting of journalists, or religious profiling — shall constitute a serious breach of this Treaty and trigger the consequences under Article 16.
3. Religious language or affiliation shall under no circumstances constitute, in itself, grounds for initiating authorisation under Article 10.

## PART VI — INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

### Article 13 — National Competent Authorities

Each Party shall designate one or more independent judicial bodies as Competent Authorities under this Treaty, with security of tenure for their members and operational independence from the executive branch. Competent Authorities shall publish annual transparency reports including the number of authorisations issued, refused, and reviewed.

### Article 14 — International Oversight Panel

1. An International Oversight Panel (the "Panel") is hereby established, composed of nine members serving six-year non-renewable terms, appointed by consensus of the Parties from among individuals of recognised expertise in law, technology, and human rights.
2. The Panel shall have authority to:
   - review all authorisations issued under Articles 10 and 11;
   - receive complaints from individuals who believe their rights under this Treaty have been violated;
   - conduct unannounced audits of national Competent Authorities;
   - issue binding recommendations and, in cases of systemic violation, refer matters to the dispute resolution mechanism under Article 16.
3. The Panel shall report annually to the Assembly of Parties and to the public.

### Article 15 — Technology Review Board

A Technology Review Board, operating under the Panel, shall assess emerging technologies on a rolling basis and recommend treaty amendments or interpretive guidance where new capabilities create risks not addressed by existing provisions. The Board shall include independent technical experts and civil society representatives.

---

## PART VII — COMPLIANCE AND DISPUTE RESOLUTION

### Article 16 — Consequences of Breach

1. A Party found by the Panel to be in systemic breach of this Treaty shall be subject to:
   - suspension of data-sharing privileges with other Parties;
   - referral to relevant international bodies;
   - publication of a formal breach finding.
2. Individual victims of unlawful data collection shall have a right to effective remedy under the national law of the responsible Party, including compensation and deletion of unlawfully obtained data.

### Article 17 — Amendment

This Treaty may be amended by a two-thirds majority of the Parties at a Review Conference convened no sooner than five years after entry into force, and thereafter at intervals of no more than five years.

---

## PART VIII — FINAL PROVISIONS

### Article 18 — Signature, Ratification, and Accession

This Treaty is open for signature by all States. It shall enter into force upon ratification by thirty States, including at least five States representing each inhabited continent.

### Article 19 — Reservations

No reservations to this Treaty are permitted with respect to Articles 4, 10, 10a, 10b, 10c, 11, or 12.

### Article 20 — Authentic Texts

This Treaty is equally authentic in Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, and Spanish.
